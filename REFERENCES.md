# References

Every bundled model carries its own `sources` block in frontmatter, with resolvable links,
and cites them inline with `[^footnotes]`. Start there — this file is the shared background,
not the citations.

**Attribution.** The 21 bundled models are original text with their own primary sources
(Goldratt on constraints, Galton on regression, Tversky and Kahneman on framing, Schumpeter
on creative destruction, and so on). The *idea* of a cross-disciplinary latticework is
Munger's, and an earlier version of this repo shipped 98 models whose list and category
structure followed Farnam Street's *Great Mental Models* closely, without per-model
citations. Those were removed in 2.0.0 and replaced. The debt to Munger for the framing, and
to Farnam Street for popularising the individual models, stands.

## The latticework idea

- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** — Charlie Munger. https://www.stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack
  The source for the latticework approach this skill is built on: worldly wisdom as models
  from many disciplines rather than one.
- **Psychology of Human Misjudgment** — Charlie Munger (1995 speech; expanded in *Poor
  Charlie's Almanack*). Behind `bias-from-incentives`, and the direct source for the claim
  that incentive-caused bias is the most underrated of the biases.
- **Seeking Wisdom: From Darwin to Munger** — Peter Bevelin. A structured tour of Munger's
  thinking with extensive citations.

## Popular treatments

- **The Great Mental Models (Volumes 1–4)** — Shane Parrish & Rhiannon Beaubien, Farnam
  Street. The most complete written treatment of the individual models.
- **Farnam Street — Mental Models guide** — https://fs.blog/mental-models/. Free web
  companion; a good per-model entry point.
