A chemical reaction that releases energy still will not start until something supplies an
initial input — the activation energy, the barrier between the reactants and the reaction
[^arrhenius]. Wood in air is thermodynamically desperate to burn, and sits there indefinitely
without a match.

The transfer is direct: many changes that would obviously pay off never start, and the reason
is not that the payoff is doubted. It is that the barrier at the beginning is higher than the
motivation available on any given day. Writing tests pays for itself; setting up the test
harness costs an afternoon nobody has.

The intervention that follows is what makes the model useful. A catalyst lowers the barrier
rather than adding energy, and is not consumed doing it [^catalysis]. Applied to people and
organisations: stop trying to raise motivation, which is expensive and decays, and lower the
barrier instead, which is usually cheap and permanent. Prepared templates, defaults, a
scheduled slot, one person who has already done it once.

## When to Avoid

- **When the reaction is not favourable.** Lowering the barrier to a bad idea just gets you
  the bad idea faster. Check the payoff is real before optimising the start.
- **When the barrier is doing useful work.** Friction on destructive or irreversible actions
  is a safety feature. Not every barrier should come down.
- **When the true problem is sustained effort, not starting.** Some things are easy to begin
  and hard to continue, and this model points you at exactly the wrong end.
- **When repeated failed starts are the signal.** If something never sticks despite low
  barriers, the constraint is elsewhere — usually the payoff is not what you assumed.

## Thinking Steps

1. **Confirm the change is actually worth it.** State the ongoing payoff. If it is not clearly
   positive, stop here.
2. **Measure the gap between starting cost and running cost.** If starting is much harder than
   continuing, this is an activation-energy problem.
3. **Enumerate what stands between here and the first step.** Be concrete and unglamorous:
   accounts to create, permissions to request, a decision nobody has made, a blank file.
4. **Remove or pre-pay barriers.** Templates, defaults, a pre-booked hour, a working example
   to copy. Do the setup once for everyone.
5. **Find a catalyst.** A person or artifact that lowers the barrier repeatedly without being
   consumed — the colleague who has done it before, the scaffold command.
6. **Shrink the first step until it is trivially small.** The goal is not a good first step,
   it is one too small to resist.

## Coaching Questions

- "If this is obviously worth doing, why hasn't it started?"
- "What's the very first physical action, and what's in the way of it?"
- "Are we trying to raise motivation when we could lower the barrier?"
- "Who has done this before, and can they do the setup once for everyone?"
- "What's the smallest version that still counts as started?"
