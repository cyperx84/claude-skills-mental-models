# Mental Models

Reasoning frames for working through a problem. Each concept states the idea and its
sources, the conditions under which it misleads you, the steps to apply it, and questions
to ask. Pick two to four from different areas — a single-area pick leaves blind spots
unchecked.

* [Activation Energy](activation-energy.md) - Change needs an upfront input larger than its running cost, so the barrier to starting is the thing to attack.
* [Asymmetric Warfare](asymmetric-warfare.md) - The weaker side wins by refusing the stronger side's terms and changing what counts as winning.
* [Bias from Incentives](bias-from-incentives.md) - People reach the conclusions their incentives point toward, sincerely and without noticing.
* [Bottlenecks](bottlenecks.md) - A system's throughput is set by its single tightest constraint; work spent anywhere else is wasted.
* [Confirmation Bias](confirmation-bias.md) - We search for and weight evidence that supports what we already believe, and the search feels neutral from inside.
* [Creative Destruction](creative-destruction.md) - Growth arrives by destroying the thing that currently works, and incumbents defend the thing being destroyed.
* [Emergence](emergence.md) - Aggregates have properties their parts don't, so understanding the components does not give you the whole.
* [Feedback Loops](feedback-loops.md) - Output that re-enters as input — reinforcing loops accelerate, balancing loops resist, and delay makes both unstable.
* [First-Principles Thinking](first-principles-thinking.md) - Strip a problem to what you know is true, then rebuild upward without borrowing anyone's conclusions.
* [Framing](framing.md) - The same facts presented differently produce different decisions — so the frame is a choice someone made.
* [Inertia](inertia.md) - Things keep doing what they were doing; change requires a force, and mass determines how much.
* [Inversion](inversion.md) - Approach a goal backward by asking what would guarantee failure, then avoid those things.
* [Leverage](leverage.md) - Find the point where a small input produces a disproportionate output, and apply force there.
* [Margin of Safety](margin-of-safety.md) - Build for materially worse than your best estimate, because your estimate is an estimate.
* [Randomness](randomness.md) - Much of what looks like signal is noise, and we build causal stories over both without noticing.
* [Regression to the Mean](regression-to-the-mean.md) - Extreme results are followed by less extreme ones for statistical reasons, independent of anything you did.
* [Sampling](sampling.md) - Small and self-selected samples lie confidently, and the people you can hear from are rarely the ones you need.
* [Scarcity](scarcity.md) - Not having enough of something captures attention and degrades judgement about everything else.
* [Second-Order Thinking](second-order-thinking.md) - Ask "and then what?" at least twice — the first-order winner is often the second-order loser.
* [Social Proof](social-proof.md) - We infer correct behaviour from what others do, most strongly exactly when we are least certain.
* [Trade-offs](trade-offs.md) - The real cost of a choice is the best thing you gave up to make it, not the money you spent.
